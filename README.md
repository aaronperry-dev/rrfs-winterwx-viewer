# RRFS winter-weather dashboard · New England

A static GitHub Pages dashboard for the NOAA **Rapid Refresh Forecast System (RRFS) ensemble** over New England, built around
everything that matters in a winter storm: QPF, snowfall by ten snowfall methods plus a method-spread map, freezing rain and sleet, precipitation type,
dendritic-growth-zone (DGZ) diagnostics, lift, wind, visibility, blizzard criteria and wind chill.

It looks and behaves like the sibling RTMA/URMA viewer (same layout, colours, station values and map furniture), and is
regenerated by GitHub Actions a few times a day. **No data is ever committed to git**: each run builds the site in a scratch
directory and deploys it as a Pages artifact.

## What you can do on the page

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Topic / Product** | 7 topics, 30 products (table below). |
| **Ensemble view** | `RRFS Mean (REFS)` · `Min` · `P5` · `P10` · `P25` · `P50` · `Mean` · `PMM` · `LPMM` · `SAM` · `SAM–LPM` · `P75` · `P90` · `P95` · `Max` · `Spread (σ)` · `Probability`, for every product (`PMM` and `LPMM` only for precipitation amounts, `SAM` and `SAM–LPM` only for liquid and snowfall amounts, see below). Percentiles are linearly interpolated across the members switched on, so with five members P5/P10 sit near the minimum and P90/P95 near the maximum. |
| **PMM / LPMM** | Probability-matched mean (Ebert 2001): the member-mean field supplies the spatial pattern and its values are replaced by the pooled member values of equal rank, which restores the amplitude the plain mean smooths away. LPMM (Clark 2017) does this within local patches, so distant regimes do not set the amounts. Implemented patch-wise as in Snook et al. (2020): patches are one third of the calculation domain; choose 120, 180 (default: 60 km patches, 180 km domain) or 240 km. Offered only for the precipitation-amount products (QPF, the snowfall products, freezing rain and sleet, and their 1-hour views), where averaging smears out the maxima PMM restores; ratios, temperature, wind, p-type probabilities and the rest keep the plain mean (switching to such a product while a PMM view is up falls back to the member mean). Computed in the browser from the members switched on (global PMM ~0.1 s, LPMM ~0.5 s of CPU per map); also shown in the *All members* layout. The heavy work runs in Web Workers (up to 4, in parallel), so the page stays responsive; results are cached and the next hours are computed ahead of time while you look at the current one, so stepping and playing are instant after the first map. With only five members the pooled distribution is coarse. |
| **SAM / SAM–LPM / Position (experimental)** | Spatially aligned mean (Lee, Brewster, Snook, Spencer & Park 2024). Each member is moved by a smooth displacement field (member-to-mean alignment toward the PMM, O(members)), resampled by interpolation, and given back its own original value distribution (so interpolation does not shave its maxima). **SAM** is the average of the aligned members; **SAM–LPM** applies the localized probability-matched mean (same 120/180/240 km selector as LPMM) to the aligned members; **Position** maps the root-mean-square displacement of the members in km (how far they had to move to line up; blank where no member has precipitation), a direct measure of location disagreement. Offered for QPF and the snowfall products and their 1-hour views (not freezing rain or sleet). **Cold-season adaptations (ours, not from the paper, not yet verified):** (1) the displacements are found on the model's liquid-equivalent precipitation over the same window and applied to every snowfall product, so a rain/snow line is not mistaken for a displaced feature and all snowfall methods of a member move together; (2) 1 h and 3 h views use the displacement of the 6 h ending at the valid time, so consecutive frames do not jitter; (3) amounts under 0.01 in take no part in the matching (the broad trace-amount shield would otherwise dominate the cost); if QPF is missing for a member the product's own field is used with a 0.1 in floor. The displacement is found in two passes on a 4x coarser (12 km) square-root copy: 600 km patches (search ±96 km), then 225 km patches (±60 km) on the residual; patches overlap by half and their shifts are averaged with tent weights; each patch minimises the squared difference times the SOAR penalty exp(r)/(1+r), r = shift/λ, λ = 0.1·patch diagonal; sub-cell shifts come from a parabola through the cost minimum. Choices the paper does not give (coarsening, square-root transform, penalty strength, search ranges, quantile-matching the member to the target before searching) are ours, so this is SAM-*style*, not a replication. About 0.3 s of CPU per member, in the same Web Workers, cached in two stages like PMM. **Read it with care:** alignment hides location uncertainty (use *Probability* or *Position*); each aligned member keeps its own domain total, so the SAM map has the plain mean's domain total, but amounts are moved locally (point and basin totals change), and SAM–LPM, like LPMM, does not keep exact totals; the paper verified it on warm-season convective rainfall (6-h, 1-25 mm), not on snowfall or banding; with fewer than 3 members there is little to align. Still to try, needing a back-test on observed snowfall (NOHRSC analyses): aligning terrain-detrended amounts, a finer third pass (~100 km) for bands, a 120 km LPM neighbourhood as default. |
| **Members** | Click `m001`–`m005` to switch members **on or off**; mean, spread, min, max and probabilities are recomputed instantly in your browser from the members left on. |
| **Single member** | Look at one member on its own. |
| **Probability of** | Threshold buttons per product (e.g. ≥ 6 in of Kuchera snow, ≥ 0.25 in QPF, ≥ 50 mph gust, ≤ 0 °F wind chill). |
| **Accumulation window** | **1 h**, 3 h, 6 h, 12 h, 24 h or run total, for QPF and every snowfall method. 1-h maps are the hour ending at the valid time (a stored field, so the maps step every 3 h); 3/6/12/24-h windows are differences of stored totals. |
| **All members** layout | Postage-stamp grid: REFS mean, member mean, spread, probability and every member side by side. |
| **Percentiles** layout | All ten statistics (Min, P5, P10, P25, P50, Mean, P75, P90, P95, Max) side by side for the current product, window and time; click one to open it. |
| **Time** | Timeline bar in the style of the SPC HREF viewer: day labels, one `Fnnn` cell per forecast hour (click or drag), a floating box with the valid time in UTC, `< ▶ >` buttons and a `- SPEED: n +` control. Keys: `<` `>` or ← → step, `p` or space plays, `h` hides the top menu, `s` saves the map as a PNG. Hover a cell for UTC and Eastern times. |
| **Zoom and pan** | Scroll wheel (about the cursor), the **+ / − / Full** buttons, keys `+` `-` `0`, double-click, pinch on touch screens; drag to pan (or `shift` + arrows). Works for every product and ensemble view, and the small-multiple views (members, percentiles) follow the same zoom. The zoom is kept in the link (`zoom=2.50,0.44,0.44`), so a zoomed view can be shared. Values are **interpolated bilinearly**, and only then put into the colour classes, so no colour is invented and the class edges follow the interpolated field (bilinear stays inside the range of the four surrounding cells, so it creates no false maxima or minima). The **Cells** button shows the nearest 3 km cell instead, for checking what the model actually has; the readout under the pointer and the station labels always give the value of the cell itself. Past 1× the state, county, coast and lake lines are drawn as vectors (`data/lines.bin`, same colours and widths as the overlay PNGs) so they stay sharp, and more station labels appear as there is room (same priority rule and spacing in screen pixels). The full view (1×) is the unchanged image: the page draws it exactly as before. **Speed:** a repaint is one pass over the visible pixels (about 15–25 ms on a software-rendered browser, with no GPU), coalesced to one per animation frame however many wheel or pointer events arrive. The stroked boundary lines and highways are kept as a raster for the current view, so stepping through the hours while zoomed (or drawing the many small maps of the stamp layouts) only repaints the field. The URL is updated once movement stops, because Safari refuses more than about 100 URL changes in 30 seconds. |

The link stores the view and is rewritten (without adding history entries) whenever the product, hour, view, zoom, layers or the plume location change, so it can be bookmarked or shared:

`?product=snow_kuchera&fhr=48&region=new_england&init_datetime=202611061200&valid_datetime=202611081200`

`fhr` is the forecast hour; `init_datetime` and `valid_datetime` are UTC, `YYYYMMDDHHMM`. After these come only the settings that differ from the defaults: `mode` (ensemble view), `window` (accumulation hours), `layout`, `zoom=z,cx,cy[,1]`, `layers`, the plume location (`station=KBOS` or `point=row,col`) and `plume_snow`. A link made from an older run shows the same *valid* time on the newest run (the forecast hour changes) when that time is still in the forecast; otherwise `fhr` is used. Old `#product/mode/frame/...` links still open and are rewritten into this form.

The layout follows the SPC HREF viewer: Date / Run / Sector selectors and title across the top, large topic tabs with a product bar beneath, the timeline,
then the map with a right-hand sidebar of checkbox-style options (ensemble view, accumulation window, members), view buttons, keyboard shortcuts and links.

## Snow growth (DGZ): the science and the sources

* **Why −12 to −18 °C.** Crystal shape depends on temperature and on supersaturation with respect to ice: plates near −2 °C, columns and needles near −5 °C, thin plates and
  stellar dendrites near −15 °C, columns again near −30 °C, with structures getting more branched as supersaturation grows (the Nakaya diagram, Libbrecht 2012).
  Growth by vapour deposition is fastest near −15 °C, so forecasters take −12 to −18 °C as the dendritic growth zone. Unrimed dendrites and plates give the
  highest snow-to-liquid ratios; riming lowers them, often to 10:1 or less (WPC snow forecasting guide; NWS VLab Forecast Operations Guide).
* **Ratio versus temperature.** The Cobb & Waldstreicher curve (2011 version, as NBM and SREF use it) gives layer ratios of about 26:1 at −16 °C, 22.5:1 at −14 °C,
  17.5:1 at −12 °C, 23:1 at −18 °C, 18:1 at −20 °C, 12:1 at −22 °C and 12:1 at −10 °C, falling to about 9:1 near −6 °C and 7:1 below −26 °C: a broad peak around the
  DGZ, not a cliff at its edges. That curve is what the "Cobb snowfall" products use.
* **Moisture and ascent.** The NWS MDL SLR method counts layers with relative humidity above 90 % with respect to ice and rising motion. The usual rule of thumb is
  RH of at least about 80 % over water for dendritic growth, and ascent inside the DGZ should be assessed: stronger ascent in a moist DGZ produces more snow and
  bands (DTN; NWS Bismarck winter diagnostics "Dendritic RH and Omega" and "OPRH"). Roebber et al. found temperature the stronger control on snow density and humidity
  secondary.
* **Depth.** The usual forecaster's reading is that a deep moist DGZ gives crystals more time to grow as they fall and a very shallow one limits dendritic growth;
  NWS Western Region technical attachment 16-04 (Uttech) examines DGZ depth and humidity against observed ratios in one storm.
* **Warm layers.** A warm nose or a warm layer near 0 °C keeps ratios low and can melt snow into sleet or freezing rain; see the Warm nose and precipitation-type maps.

## Data and the "lean fetch"

* Bucket `noaa-rrfs-ops-pds` (anonymous, `us-east-1`):
  * members `rrfsens.YYYYMMDD/CC/m00N/rrfs.tCCz.m00N.{2dfldnomads,prslevnomads}.3km.fFFF.conus.grib2`
  * REFS mean `refs.YYYYMMDD/CC/ensprod/refs.tCCz.mean.fFF.conus.grib2`
  * REFS probability-matched mean `refs.YYYYMMDD/CC/ensprod/refs.tCCz.pmmn.fFF.conus.grib2` (`REFC`, `REFD`; optional: if it is missing the reflectivity layers just show nothing from REFS)
* Messages that sit back to back in a file (the four precipitation-type flags, runs of pressure levels) are fetched in **one** range request (capped at 8 MB), which roughly halves the request count with exactly the same bytes (11 wanted messages = 6 requests in a member 2dfld file, 26 = 13 in a prslev file). A build used to make about 7,500 requests (about 1,300 per member) and moves about 2.4 GB; merging removes roughly half of the member requests; at most 16 run at once per process (4 processes), far below S3's 5,500 GET/s per prefix, and botocore's standard retry mode backs off on any `SlowDown` answer.
* Every GRIB2 file has a `.idx` sidecar. The code reads it (a few kB), finds the byte range of each needed message and issues one
  HTTP `Range` request per field, a ~1–2 MB message instead of a multi-hundred-MB file. Messages are decoded from memory with
  `eccodes`; nothing is written to disk.
* Per member and 3-hourly frame: 3 hours of `APCP` and type flags, `ASNOW`, 2 m / 10 m fields, `GUST`, `VIS`, `PRES`, `MAXUVV`,
  `TMP` on 13 pressure levels (1000–300 hPa) and `RH` on 13 (the three lowest, 1000/975/950 hPa, are for the DGZ only) plus 2 m `RH` → roughly 80 requests including index files. Once per cycle the deterministic `prslev` file's `DZDT` on 26 levels is fetched per frame (about 20 MB, 26 requests) and shared with the members through a scratch file, which is removed at the end of the build (`--no-lift` skips it). The five members and the REFS mean run in parallel processes.
* The newest cycle (00/06/12/18 UTC) whose **last forecast hour (F060)** exists for the REFS mean and every member is used.
* Fields are re-projected from the 3 km Lambert grid onto a fixed New England raster (3 km, nearest neighbour, indices
  pre-computed and cached), then quantised (8/16 bit) and gzip-compressed per product and source. The browser decodes them with
  the native `DecompressionStream`. Each file starts with a mode byte: the fields are stored as a 2-D spatial delta, and, where a cheap
  bit-cost estimate says it is smaller (running totals such as QPF and snowfall: the increment between frames is much sparser than the
  total), also as a delta between frames; 16-bit fields are then byte-shuffled. Both together roughly halve the gzip size, and the
  frame delta takes a further ~30% off the 16-bit files in the offline demo.

### Why the member mean is computed in the browser

The page needs to be able to switch members on and off. Pre-rendering every combination would be 2⁵ × products × frames images,
which is far too much for GitHub Actions and Pages. Instead the server stores one compact file per product and member, and the
browser reduces whichever members are switched on. That keeps the build small, makes any combination instant, and means no
re-computation of products on the server.

### REFS mean vs. the member mean

They are not the same thing. NOAA's REFS mean is computed over **all** REFS members (the 12 RRFS members, plus time-lagged members and two HRRR members), but only
five members are on S3 and the dashboard's member mean covers only those. The REFS mean file also carries far fewer fields than the
members (no gusts, no pressure-level profiles). Fields it lacks are shown as unavailable in the `RRFS Mean (REFS)` view and
fall back to the member mean with a notice.

## Basemap: terrain and water

Two static images are rendered **once** (`rrfs_viewer/terrain.py`), in the same Lambert frame as the boundary overlay, and published as `terrain/basemap.<hash>.webp` (opaque RGB, 3450 × 2226) and `terrain/relief.<hash>.webp` (RGBA, 1725 × 1113). Their names carry a content hash, so browsers keep them between the four daily builds.

* **Elevation and depth**: the public AWS *Terrain Tiles* (`elevation-tiles-prod`, "terrarium" encoding; SRTM, USGS 3DEP/NED, GMTED, ETOPO1, GEBCO), zoom 9 (about 220 m per pixel at 44 °N, ~430 tiles, 40-70 MB, downloaded **once** and cached in `.cache/terrain_tiles`). They carry sea-floor depths and the Great Lakes' bathymetry; smaller lakes are flat in them.
* **Land, ocean, lake outlines**: Natural Earth 1:10m `land`, `lakes` and `lakes_north_america`, the same data as the coast and lake lines of the overlay, so fills and lines agree. Land / lake / ocean are separated with anti-aliased masks.
* **Land**: an OKLab elevation ramp (pale sage at sea level to a warm grey at ~1,950 m, the highest summits in the frame) times a **multi-directional hillshade** (sun mostly from the north-west, 40° high, vertical exaggeration 3, because New England relief is low). Shadows are limited so no part of the map gets dark.
* **Oceans**: a depth ramp, pale at the coast to a grey-blue at 4,000 m, spaced on √depth so the shelf gets the resolution. **Lakes**: their own, slightly greener ramp, by depth below the lake's surface (surface = the 90th percentile of the elevation inside the lake polygon, so a lake that is flat in the elevation data is a single shallow tone).
* **Relief over the data**: the same hillshade as black (shadow) or white (lit) alpha on land, and a blue tint on water growing with the square root of the depth; drawn with canvas `source-atop`, i.e. only where the field has colour, at 0.3 / 0.55 / 1 of the amplitude baked into the image.
* **Colours stay out of the way**: all ramps are low in chroma (`tests/test_terrain.py` checks that the lightness only decreases with elevation / depth, and that the chroma stays small). A near-white or light-blue data class is still clearly distinct from the basemap, but if you want it paler or stronger, the constants at the top of `terrain.py` (`LAND_RAMP`, `OCEAN_RAMP`, `LAKE_RAMP`, `BASE_*`, `RELIEF_*`, `OCEAN_*`, `LAKE_*`, `VERT_EXAG`, `SUN_*`) are the only place to change; the tiles are cached, so re-rendering takes about 15 s.
* **Caching**: a render is cached under `.cache/terrain/<key>/` (the key is a hash of `terrain.py` and the frame), and the Actions cache keeps `.cache` between runs (the cache key includes `terrain.py`). To pre-render the assets and commit them instead (no tile download on CI at all): `python -m rrfs_viewer.terrain` writes `rrfs_viewer/data/terrain/`; the build uses those files while their key still matches the code (`--force` re-renders).
* **If it cannot be made**: the tiles unreachable gives a *flat* basemap (land tint, ocean and lake colours, no hillshade or depth; not cached, so the next build tries again); any other failure leaves the old white background, and the page copes with a missing `basemap` in `data.json`. `--no-terrain` skips the step.
* **Demo**: `--demo` renders an *invented* landscape (`testing.SyntheticTerrain`: the real coast and summits, invented relief), so the build stays offline. It is only for checking the page and is not geography.
* **Attribution** (shown on the page, in the footer and in the PNG): Mapzen / AWS Terrain Tiles (USGS, NOAA, GEBCO, SRTM); Natural Earth.

## Size

Measured on the offline demo (455 × 293 display grid, 4 frames, 6 sources, 37 stored series including the 1-hour ones): 19 MB of data
files + a 46 kB overlay. Scaled to the 20 frames of a full run that is roughly 95 MB for the whole site (synthetic fields; real fields
may compress differently). Before the frame delta was added, a 24 h demo build (8 frames) wrote 45.8 MB of data files; with it, 35.2 MB (-23%;
-30% on the 16-bit QPF/snowfall files, -5% on the 8-bit ones). Real fields are noisier, so check `stats.data_bytes` in `data.json` after the first live run. The basemap adds about 1-2 MB of static images (two WebP files, cached by content hash). A visitor downloads only the product being viewed, and 1-hour fields only when the 1 h window is chosen:
about 3-6 MB for all six sources, cached afterwards.
What a build **downloads** from S3 is much larger than what it publishes (every needed message at full CONUS resolution). The first live
run's REFS-mean pass fetched 73 MB; expect a few GB for the five members, which is free on a public repository but is the thing to
check in the Actions log.

## GitHub Actions limits

Designed so that scheduled generation is never stopped by GitHub:

* The repository is **public** (Actions minutes are free), runs finish in minutes, the job has a hard `timeout-minutes`, and a
  `concurrency` group prevents overlap.
* Data is never committed; the site is built in the runner and deployed with `upload-pages-artifact` + `deploy-pages`. The
  deployed site is on the order of 100 MB (see *Size*), far under the 1 GB Pages limit, and the repo itself stays tiny.
* Runs are scheduled about 3 h 50 min after each cycle (03:50, 09:50, 15:50, 21:50 UTC), with retries at +4 h 30 and +5 h 30 for slow cycles. A run that finds the site already showing the newest complete cycle
  exits in about a minute without deploying (`--skip-if-deployed`).
* GitHub disables scheduled workflows in public repos after 60 days without repository activity. A best-effort step re-enables
  the workflow on every run. If scheduled runs stop anyway, push any commit or press *Run workflow*.

## Set up

1. Create a public repository, e.g. `rrfs-viewer`, and push this folder.
2. **Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Source: GitHub Actions.**
3. **Actions → Update RRFS winter dashboard → Run workflow** (tick *force* the first time).

The site appears at `https://<user>.github.io/<repo>/`. The first run also downloads the elevation tiles (once, see *Basemap*) and takes a minute or two longer.

## Limitations: read before trusting a number

* **Reflectivity is model-simulated, not radar.** The real member files carry composite reflectivity (`REFC`) only (checked against the 2dfld `.idx` in `tests/data`); 1 km reflectivity (`REFD`) is documented for the REFS probability-matched mean (NWS notice SCN26-048) but its exact `.idx` level wording could not be checked from here, so it is matched loosely (`1000 m above ground`). If the build log shows `REFD:` under `field not found`, look at the `hints` for the wording the file really uses. Reflectivity is stored in 0.4 dBZ steps with every value below 0 dBZ set to a no-echo floor.

* **Accumulation windows are auto-detected.** For `APCP`, `WEASD` and `ASNOW` the code reads each file's `.idx` and uses run totals, 1-hour or longer tiling windows, whichever the file has. If a field is still not found, the Actions log prints a `field not found … ->` line listing what the file does contain.
* **Partly validated against the live bucket.** The field names and `.idx` descriptors (`APCP` 1-hour windows, `ASNOW` totals, `MAXUVV`,
  `VIS`, pressure-level `TMP`/`RH`) follow the published RRFS/REFS product tables and the layout of comparable NCEP files; they were
  developed offline; the first live run found the REFS-mean precipitation windows were not as assumed (now handled adaptively). The Actions log lists what was missing (`field not found … ->` lines and the `missing=` counts per source).
* **Missing data shrinks a product, it does not blank it.** The build and the page use whatever members, frames and hours exist:
  * A source (member) that fails outright is logged and left out; the other members are published. If no cycle is complete for every source, the newest cycle with at least three finished members is used (sources that have begun publishing are used, missing frames stay empty); the site is rebuilt once the rest has finished.
  * **Model snowfall (`ASNOW`)** uses the run total when the file has one, otherwise a window that covers the new 3 h, otherwise the 1-hour windows summed. A frame whose run total is missing is bridged (next bullet); a later run total repairs anything before it.
  * **Hourly QPF hole:** the missing liquid is recovered from the run-total `APCP` when the file has one; if not, the totals are *lower bounds*. If the precipitation-type flags of an hour are missing, the previous hour's type is used; a snow method that lacks a ratio for a frame uses its last one.
  * **Empty accumulation frames** (up to two in a row, 6 h) between two good ones are filled by linear interpolation in time; accumulations only grow and are smooth at 3 h spacing. Leading holes are bridged from zero; trailing holes stay empty.
  * `data.json` carries `quality[member][frame]`, a bit mask: 1 some hours estimated, 2 lower bound (hours missing), 4 frame interpolated, 8 nothing. The page names the affected members in the yellow notice under the map and marks those hours with open circles on the plumes and in their tooltip. A member with no data for a product at a frame is left out of that frame's mean, percentiles and probabilities and named in the notice.
  * These are estimates and say so; they cannot be checked against the live bucket from the development sandbox. They were tested on the offline fake S3 with messages removed (`tests/test_pipeline.py`); `RRFS_DEMO_HOLES="m003:ASNOW:48,m004:APCP:23"` with `--demo` builds a demo site with holes in single members.
* **Zoomed maps show the same 3 km data, not more detail.** Interpolation only makes the shading smooth; it adds no information, and
  features smaller than a few grid cells are not resolved however far you zoom. The display raster is a nearest-neighbour resample of the native
  grid, so a "cell" can sit up to about 1.5 km from where the model's own cell is. Boundary lines are Natural Earth 1:10m (about 1:10 million scale),
  so at high zoom they are generalised, and so are the optional highways (a road can sit a kilometre or more from where it really is). The city layer is Natural Earth's short list (28 places in the six states); smaller places come from the Towns layer (GeoNames, 1,000 people or more, so very small villages are absent), and the peak list is a selection of prominent summits, not every named peak. The *New England land area* summary tiles and the point chart always use the whole domain.
* **Snowfall methods are guidance, not forecasts.** A ratio is applied to model QPF, so all of them inherit its errors. The ratio at the
  end of each 3-hour block is used for the whole block. There are two thickness methods: the CMC/Murphy relation, which only *lowers* the
  ratio in marginal, warm-layer snow, and the NBM line, which also raises it in cold layers (about 15:1 at 1500 m, 23:1 at 1450 m). The
  NBM document does not state the thickness unit; metres is the only reading that gives sensible ratios, and its clip limits are ours.
* **Cobb: two versions, and neither has the members' own lift.** Cobb's method weights each layer by `sqrt(VV) × RH²/6400`; the public
  RRFS member files carry no pressure-level vertical velocity. *Cobb snowfall* sets `VV` to its floor of 1 (as NBM does for members without
  it), so the weights are humidity only. *Cobb snowfall (with lift)* takes `VV` from the **deterministic** RRFS `prslev` file, which has `DZDT`
  (geometric w, m/s) every 25 hPa from 925 to 300 hPa, and combines it with each member's own temperature and humidity. The deterministic run is
  a different forecast, so where it puts the ascent somewhere other than the member does, the ratio is wrong in the same way; it is one more
  estimate, not the full method for the member. Details: `omega = -rho g w` with `rho` from the member's temperature, `VV` in microbar/s,
  floor 1 for downward, missing or weak lift. **The microbar/s unit is an assumption** (the NBM slides give only the floor of 1; microbar/s is
  the usual omega unit of the Cobb/BUFKIT work, and 1 microbar/s is about 1.3 cm/s of ascent at 700 hPa), so check the lift version against
  NBM or BUFKIT Cobb ratios before trusting it. The grid of the deterministic file is compared with the members' grid; if it differs, or the file
  is missing for a frame, the lift products are left empty for that run or frame instead of falling back to the no-lift ratio. A missing hour
  is handled as for the other fields (see above). Checked against real inventories (`tests/data`): the member `prslevnomads` files
  have only `HGT`, `TMP`, `RH`, `DPT`, `SPFH`, `UGRD`, `VGRD`, `ABSV`, the `2dfldnomads` files only the column maxima `MAXUVV`/`MAXDVV`, the REFS
  mean has `DZDT` at 700 mb and over 700-500 mb only, and the deterministic `prslev` file (F003, 00Z 2026-11-06) has `DZDT` on all 26 levels.
  In published tests Cobb has been mixed: it was poor in a WRF study of 14 New England nor'easters (UConn preprint), but ahead of the thickness
  and max-T-aloft methods in Pletcher et al. (2026, eastern CONUS, mean absolute error 3.52 in against 3.79 and 4.12; their random forest scored
  2.84 and linear regression 3.42). Treat both as extra estimates, not references.
  The no-lift Cobb is part of the method spread; neither Cobb is in the multi-method mean, and the lift version is not in the spread (it can be empty for runs and frames the deterministic file does not cover). Deliberately not in the dashboard: the NBM v4.2 melt adjustment (it
  raised the error in Pletcher et al.'s test), the Cobb + melt and NBM-blend products, NBM's temperature perturbation and the 2011 SREF rule that a layer must exceed 90 % humidity over ice. `winter.py` still
  has `melt_factor`, `wet_bulb_c` and `slr_blend` with their tests if you want them back (`winter.MELT_DENOM` is 15 in the August 2023 NBM-team deck
  and 10 in Cobb's March 2024 talk).
* **Not implemented.** The Ware/Roebber liquid-amount correction has no published coefficients that I could reach, only a qualitative
  nomogram; start from Roebber et al. (2003) or Ware et al. (2006) if you want it.
* **Byun is a lower bound in cold snow.** It was fitted to Korean 3-hour data and cannot exceed `a` (11.9–18.8), so in cold, dry New England
  snow it will sit well below Kuchera or the NBM thickness line. It uses the 2 m temperature at the valid time, not a 3-hour mean, and the
  ratio at the end of a 3-hour block with that block's total precipitation for all three hours.
* **The climatology is a reference.** 1971–2000 COOP data with a 2-inch snowfall minimum, so wind undercatch and settling bias it, with
  no seasonal cycle. It is interpolated between seven office means and is not terrain-aware.
* **Max-T-aloft ratio is experimental.** The polynomial was fitted to Alaska observations and is untested for New England;
  treat it as one more scenario next to Kuchera, 10:1 and the model's own `ASNOW`. The layer runs from 2000 ft AGL (≈ 610 m, as in NBM) up to
  500 hPa, the highest level used for it; NBM and Veals et al. go to 400 hPa (nothing above 500 hPa is fetched).
* **Kuchera** uses the 2 m temperature and levels up to 500 hPa (the original used the surface-to-500 hPa layer as well).
* **DGZ humidity** uses RH on 1000–500 hPa (1000, 975 and 950 hPa are fetched for the DGZ only) and the 2 m RH, which with the 2 m temperature is the bottom of
  the profile where the ground cuts between two levels, so a DGZ in the lowest part of the column (shallow cold air, high terrain) is counted. Layers with no RH at all
  count as not moist. The deterministic-lift Cobb ratio is unchanged (it starts at 925 hPa).
* **The DGZ maps are instantaneous ingredient maps, not snowfall.** They are valid at the frame time and say nothing about whether precipitation is falling or whether
  it reaches the ground as snow, so they do not have to line up with the snowfall products, which are accumulated totals (run total or the chosen window) from QPF and
  the precipitation type. Compare them with the 1-hour snowfall or reflectivity at the same valid time.
* **DGZ ascent is the deterministic run's, not the member's.** The members publish no vertical velocity on pressure levels. `DZDT` from the deterministic
  RRFS (already fetched once per cycle for the Cobb-with-lift ratio) is combined with each member's own temperature, so where the two runs disagree about where the
  ascent is, the DGZ ascent and "Moist DGZ with lift" disagree too. The 0.1 m/s threshold comes from Korolev & Mazin (2003) as relayed in the Williams radar preprint
  below, not from a verified snowfall criterion; check it before relying on it. The density used in the conversion is the ideal-gas value (no moisture term).
* **Humidity is taken as RH over water** at every temperature (as the model files label it) and converted to ice with Magnus-type saturation pressures.
* **The DGZ is necessary, not sufficient, for fluffy snow.** Observed snow-to-liquid ratios also depend on riming, aggregation, melting and sublimation under the
  cloud, and compaction (Roebber et al. 2003), and no single ratio method is most accurate (a 2023 NOAA ensemble-snowfall study of ratio choices). These maps show where the
  ingredients for dendrites are present, not a snowfall amount.
* **No snow depth change product.** The public RRFS files carry no snow depth (`SNOD`), so a true "accumulated positive snow depth change" cannot be
  computed. `ASNOW` (Model snowfall) is the closest field; it counts snow reaching the ground and does not take off later settling or melt.
* **Freezing rain and sleet are liquid equivalents.** No radial-ice/flat-ice accretion model is applied.
* **Blizzard** uses the model's visibility (no blowing snow) and 3-hourly samples: two consecutive qualifying samples are taken
  to span 3 hours. Treat it as guidance for where criteria are plausible.
* The member mean covers m001–m005 only; the control member is not on S3.
* Precipitation type is the model's categorical flag (0 or 1) at the valid time; hourly flags are averaged between consecutive hours when
  splitting accumulations by type.

* **The basemap is a backdrop, not data.** The images have 3 × the overlay's resolution, so past about 3× zoom the terrain is soft (the lines stay sharp); the elevation tiles are about 220 m per pixel, generalised, and shaded with a 3× vertical exaggeration. Lake depth appears only where the elevation tiles contain bathymetry (the Great Lakes); a lake that is flat in them gets one shallow tone, and a narrow lake between steep banks can look a little deeper than it is. Natural Earth's 1:10m lake outlines leave out small ponds, and outlines are generalised by up to a kilometre or so.
* **The relief over the data darkens or lightens colours by a few per cent.** On steep slopes the shading is visible in a data colour; use *Light* if that bothers you, and remember the legend swatches are never shaded. The station values and the hover readout are always the cell values.

```

  config.py      bucket layout, cycles, frames, display area
  s3.py          anonymous boto3, .idx parsing, one byte-range request per message
  grib.py        eccodes decode, native -> display raster mapping
  winter.py      SLR methods, DGZ, warm nose, RHi, wind chill, blizzard
  pipeline.py    per-member and REFS processing -> (frames, ny, nx) products

  colors.py      colour ramps (OKLab)

  basemap.py     cartopy overlay (boundaries, frame, and the same lines as vectors for zoomed views), land mask, METAR layout

  testing.py     offline fake S3 with real GRIB2, and invented terrain tiles (demo + tests)
site/index.html  the dashboard (single file, no dependencies)
.github/workflows/update.yml
```
